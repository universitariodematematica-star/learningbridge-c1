Unit 1 - Dreams and Goals

Lesson 3

Compare Your Dreams and Goals in Life

A. KEY VERB EXPRESSIONS AND DESCRIPTIVE ADJECTIVES

Read and study the following expressions and adjectives. Pay attention to their meanings and how they are used in context.

1. Key Verb Expressions

Expressions related to dreams, personal ambitions and responsibilities:

fulfill a dream

Meaning: To achieve something you have wanted to do for a long time.

Example: After years of hard work, Maria finally fulfilled her dream of becoming a doctor.

set a goal

Meaning: To decide on something specific that you want to achieve.

Example: Before starting her university studies, Ana set a goal to earn a scholarship.

work towards a goal

Meaning: To make continuous efforts to achieve something.

Example: Daniel is taking evening classes and saving money to work towards his goal of studying abroad.

pursue a goal

Meaning: To actively try to achieve something you consider important.

Example: Despite several difficulties, James decided to pursue his goal of opening his own business.

put something off

Meaning: To postpone or delay doing something until a later time.

Example: Laura had to put off her plans to travel because she needed to take care of her children.

share responsibilities

Meaning: To divide duties or tasks among two or more people.

Example: Dan and Sarah share responsibilities by dividing household chores and taking care of their children together.

2. Descriptive Adjectives

Adjectives used to describe goals, ambitions and expectations:

ambitious vs. modest

Ambitious: Having a strong desire to achieve something important or difficult.

Example: Becoming the director of an international organization is an ambitious career goal.

Modest: Not excessively ambitious or demanding; relatively limited in scope.

Example: Her first goal was modest: she simply wanted to improve her English conversation skills.

achievable vs. unachievable

Achievable: Possible to accomplish successfully.

Example: With a clear plan and consistent effort, completing the course in six months is an achievable goal.

Unachievable: Impossible or extremely difficult to accomplish under the existing conditions.

Example: Learning an entire language fluently in just two days is an unachievable objective.

realistic vs. unrealistic

Realistic: Based on a reasonable understanding of what is possible.

Example: Saving a small amount of money every month is a realistic way to prepare for university expenses.

Unrealistic: Not based on what is actually possible or reasonable.

Example: Expecting to become an expert without studying or practicing is unrealistic.

challenging

Meaning: Difficult in an interesting or stimulating way because it requires effort, skill or determination.

Example: Pursuing a medical career while raising a family can be challenging, but it can also be rewarding.
